### 🚦 Page 3: Local Traffic Improvement Delivery Programme 

Cllr Kevin Lang asked whether the LTIP scheme is on track, or ahead of / behind schedule, and whether  Ward Councillors be informed on individual projects in their localities.

### 🚋 Page 9: Trams to Newhaven Outstanding Issues

Link: [Most recent project update](https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/tramstonewhaven/news/article/425/trams-to-newhaven-issues-resolution-update){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} »

Cllr Chas Booth asked what 'regular updates' on the project means in practice; and with ninety 'red' issues remaining, what the timetable for the resolution of the most pressing defects looks like.

Officers responded that 27 of the remaining 'red' issues are already in progress, with a number of the 'soft landscaping' works remaining waiting for the planting season to come around; the deadline for all 'red' items to be completed is the 31st of March 2026. Regarding updates, there is a project update every two months by Newsletter, with an update to the number of issues on the project's website every month.

Cllr Booth then asked about the pedestrian and cycle crossing wait times over London Road at the top of Elm Row, where with the reintroduction of the left turn for vehicles from Leith Walk along London Rd, users of the crossing are facing [up to a ten minute wait](https://bsky.app/profile/edtiss.bsky.social/post/3m5h7q5vdys2j){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"}. Officers response was that they will investigate the issue, and circulate findings to Ward Councillors.

## 🗳️ Items for Decision

### 🚌 7.4 Bus Lanes and Bus Gates – Consideration of Permitting Access to Private Hire Vehicles 
📄 [Report](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/s90337/7.4%20-%20Bus%20Lanes%20and%20Bus%20Gates%20-%20Consideration%20of%20permitting%20access%20to%20PHVs.pdf){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} [PDF] »
📺 [Webcast](https://edinburgh.public-i.tv/core/portal/webcast_interactive/1035540){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} - starting from **2h23m**

Deputations (which are taken at the very start of the meeting) from a number of parties on this item, each presenting both written submissions and in person on the day:
- 🚌 **Edinburgh Bus Users Group**, written [page 6](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27129/Deputations%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=6){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"}, verbally from **22m05s**
- 🚲 **Spokes** Planning Group, [page 12](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27129/Deputations%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=12){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"}, verbally from **25m 56s**
- 🚙 Private Hire trade body, the **Scottish Private Hire Association** on [page 13](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27129/Deputations%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=13){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"}, verbally from **28m 10s**
- 🚕 **GMB Union** representing both black cab and private hire drivers [page 9](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27129/Deputations%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=9){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"}, verbally from **32m 4s**

Deputations began with an erudite summary from the Edinburgh Bus Users Group ('EBUG'), whose written deputation (linked above) gives great context to the matter at hand and speaks to the impacts of vehicle traffic in bus lanes on public transport patronage.

Following EBUG, Alex from [Spokes](http://spokes.org.uk){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} gave a short verbal deputation highlighting that the number of Private Hire Cars ('PHCs') in the city is currently uncapped, and has increased by 40% since 2018. He also mentioned for consideration the slow progress prioritising public transport and active travel infrastructure in recent years.

> _I made the rash assumption that the verbal deputations from the taxi and private hire bodies were in favour of PHCs in bus lanes as per their written submissions, and haven't watched them. Tempus fugit._

There were a couple of questions for Spokes after the verbal deputations. 

At **38m 25s** Cllr Chas Booth asked about the number of Private Hire Cars being uncapped, and whether it was thought that the Council should have a conversation about this.

Alex responded that he believed the licencing committee may be looking at this - and that a conversation around this would make sense: as the Council looks to lower vehicle kilometers driven in the city, allowing an uncapped number of hire vehicles that drive a higher than average distance in a day makes a difference.

Cllr Lesley Macinnes asked a question at **39m 12s** regarding safety - could Spokes  expand on the potential impact of having more vehicles in bus lanes for people cycling around the city; Alex responded that "Additional vehicles make spaces more uncomfortable" not only in bus lanes, but also through bus gates for people cycling or walking, and that where we've seen vehicle numbers reduced by such measures, we wouldn't want to see those increase again and make those spaces less pleasant for people walking and cycling.

The debate followed later, from **2h 23m** onwards in the webcast. 

Presenting holding position here - workstreams going forward in a number of key arteries - Lothian Rd, George St, Dalry Rd to name a few - and a proposed 7-7-7 trial period, so a lot of change in the city hence the recommendation in the report to hold off on the potential introduction of PHC vehicles into bus lanes while the impacts of those projects is assessed.

Cllr Lang asked - what is, from officers perspective, the principal argument black cabs should be allowed but PHCs should not. 
Historic decision, different legislation covering both types - it's acknowledged that PHCs play a part in the transport network, but numbers are the issue and the need to protect bus lanes for buses.
Lang - if it's numbers, why wouldn't you remove the right of black cabs to go in bus lanes.
It could be something we consider - all officers would need to consider, as black cabs are considered part of the public transport network.
Lang: What is the principle that sits behind the idea of allowing PHCs through certain bus gates?
Case by case basis depending on the bus gate - some bus gates really act as modal filters, being as they are not on a bus route. It would depend on the impacts assessed for a given location for each case in turn.
Lang: So what would be the factors taken into account case by case?
That would be a multi-officer / multi-modal conversation (officer being asked is public transport only) as to the impacts.
Bus gate destined for Market St has already been approved for PHCs.
SJ - bus gate nomenclature is difficult - these are really modal or traffic filters. We've stuck with terminology that people are familiar with but need to think about them in this respect.

Cuthbert asked about Waymo/autonomous; Macinnes asked about differences between black cabs and PHCs, and also about numbers of PHCs wheelchair accessible; answer was that there's only a handful of wheelchair-accessible PHCs in circulation but that is down to decisions made by the licencing authority rather than operators.

Amendments; [Conservative (page 10)](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27169/Motions%20and%20Amendments%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=10) and [Green (page 11)](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27169/Motions%20and%20Amendments%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9#page=11); 

In contributions, Cllr Lang made a good point in backing the continued exclusion about Public transport being above 'shared transport' - and that there is indeed a distinction (which we see in deputations, taxi and PHC firms assert that they are 'public transport' providers). 

Administration accepted Green amendment and rejected the Conservative, which cons moved anyway; 9 to 2.

### 🚌 7.6 Road Safety Delivery Plan 2025/26 – Six-month update  
📄 [Report](https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/s90335/7.6%20-%20Road%20Safety%20Delivery%20Plan%202025_26%20-%20Six%20month%20update.pdf){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} [PDF] »
📺 [Webcast](https://edinburgh.public-i.tv/core/portal/webcast_interactive/1035540){target="_blank" rel="noopen noreferrer"} - starting from **3h 32m** 

7.6 – Road Safety Delivery Plan 2025/26 – Six-month update
Deputations from Living Streets Edinburgh Group (written, page 19) and Living Rent Lochend (verbal, starting from **58m 22s**). 
Doc https://democracy.edinburgh.gov.uk/documents/b27129/Deputations%2013th-Nov-2025%2010.00%20Transport%20and%20Environment%20Committee.pdf?T=9 [PDF]

A powerful deputation from representatives of Living Rent Lochend at **58m 22s**, who recently launched a road safety campaign in the area; 

Lots to this report, and very varied questions and contributions on it.

- Road safety team has doubled in size to 8 officers this year - working to build capacity. 

Cuthbert: p416 stats - accident stats from 2014-2018? Not more up to date?
This period used nationally in Scotland as the baseline.
C: Are different modes recorded differently?
Only types not recorded are suicides. Any other accident related to pedestrians, cyclists, bus users falling onboard, tram; all recorded.

Lang: move away from Pv2, please to see - interested in the rationale in it, we've always been told it's objective and required by ScotGov, what has changed?
It's a tool and will still be used, but SG's own road safety framework we've been asked to look at the 'safe systems approach' and aim for Vision Zero; to address unmet demand, we need to modernise our approach and use a system that focuses on a community and not a spreadsheet;

Lang: School travel plans - what is the timescale for all schools having been covered by this process?
Five year process, prioritising schools without physical measures or plans already in place. We will deliver on actions already agreed but then revisit schools further down the list later on.

Vehicle activated signs that can be moved around - pavement NAL sockets at multiple locations but use of signage 'kept fresh' by rotation and reintroduction where monitoring reveals continuing issues with speed.

Q Cllr Booth following Critical Mass protest outside - Cowgate - designs by March 2026; as well as seasonal winter / summertime streets closures.
